# cold outreach for jobs

get a stranger at ur dream company to actually read (and answer) ur message. // a Hot Girls Code skill :)

**the draft or situation:** $ARGUMENTS

if that's empty, use whatever the user pasted. if there's still nothing, ask one short question: "who are you reaching out to, and for what role?"

credit: the five tips come from [Diego Zaks' LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/diegozaks_ive-received-countless-terrible-cold-outreach-share-7188176913150935040-ciC1/) on cold outreach from aspiring designers. this skill puts them into steps an agent can follow, and they work for any role, not just design.

## why this matters

the person getting the message owes u nothing, and they get a lot of these. most read the first couple of lines and decide. a short, specific, human message shows u did the work, so they're more likely to do the work of replying.

## the five tips

1. **apply first, then say so.** apply for the role before reaching out, and name it in the message. it shows ur serious and gives them something concrete to do (like flag ur application)
2. **ask one specific question.** something u actually want to know about their work, team or product. "pick ur brain", "learn more about the team" and "grab a coffee" are too vague to answer
3. **keep it to 2-4 sentences.** that's about all u get before they tune out
4. **sound like a person.** no formal openers ("i hope this message finds u well"), no buzzwords, no template energy. write the way u'd talk to someone at a meetup
5. **give something before u ask.** a relevant reference, article, bug, or small idea for their product. keep it small. no unsolicited redesigns or spec work, no one wants free labor

## before u write

1. **get the basics.** who they are, the company, the role, and whether the user has applied. if the user hasn't applied, recommend doing that first, but still write the message and leave `[applied for X role]` as a placeholder
2. **find the real question.** ask the user what they're curious about. if they don't know, look at what's in front of u (the person's role, the company's product, the job post) and suggest 2-3 specific questions to pick from. don't invent facts about the person or company
3. **find the gift.** something small and relevant the user can share. if there's nothing yet, leave `[small relevant thing]` and suggest a couple of ideas

## write it

- **sentence 1:** who the user is in a few words + the role they applied for
- **sentence 2:** the small thing they're sharing, and why it made them think of this person or product
- **sentence 3:** the one specific question
- **optional sentence 4:** a light close that's easy to answer or ignore ("no worries if ur slammed")

no "pick ur brain", no "15 min of ur time" as the main ask, no résumé paragraph, no "i hope this finds u well". if it's over 4 sentences, cut until it isn't.

match the channel. LinkedIn and X DMs stay casual. an email can have a short subject line that names the role.

## examples

- before: "Hi Maya, I hope this message finds you well! I'm a passionate product designer with 3 years of experience and I'm really interested in the work your team is doing. Would you have 15 minutes to let me pick your brain and learn more about the team?"
  after: "hey Maya, i just applied for the product designer role on ur growth team. i saw ur talk on reducing signup steps and tried it on my own side project: completion went up a bit, which surprised me. how do u decide when a step is worth cutting vs. explaining better?"
- before: "Hello, I am reaching out to express my interest in opportunities at your company. Please find my portfolio attached. I would appreciate the chance to connect."
  after: "hi Jordan, i applied for the frontend role last week. heads up, the pricing page toggle doesn't update the annual prices on safari (screenshot attached). curious: does ur team own the marketing site too, or is that separate?"

## check before u deliver

- 2-4 sentences?
- does it name the role (or have a placeholder for it)?
- is there exactly one specific question they could answer in a line or two?
- is there a small, relevant gift, and is it clearly not spec work?
- does it sound like a person said it out loud?
- did u avoid making up facts about the person or company?

deliver the message first. then add a short note: the main changes, any placeholders to fill in, a reminder to apply first if they haven't, and a reminder that if they don't hear back in a week or so, one short nudge is fine.
